An error in this week's US weekly jobless claims report shows the importance of revisions. Eagle-eyed observers noticed it almost right away — Thursday's weekly US jobless claims report stated that Connecticut had 298,680 claims for the week ending May 9. It stood out for a few reasons. The first is that Connecticut had 36,138 claims in the prior week, meaning that Thursday's number was a whopping 726% increase in just seven days. The second is that it put Connecticut at the top of the chart — the Thursday number was the highest in the nation, beating out other larger states such as New York, California, and Pennsylvania. Read more: A 20-year hedge fund vet shares the 3-part checklist that guides every investment decision he makes — and breaks down a stock pick he thinks could increase 50 to 100 times in his lifetime A few hours later, the Connecticut Department of Labor set the record straight — the lofty jobless claims number was an error, it said. "#USDOL May 14 report shows CT with 298,680 initial UI claims filed. Correct number is 29,846," the state department of labor said in a tweet. The mistake was the result of a "data entry error" that has since been corrected, Nancy Steffens, a spokesperson for the Connecticut Department of Labor, told Business Insider. That means that the total initial jobless claims in the week ending May 9 may be revised from the nearly 3 million reported Thursday in the next report.
