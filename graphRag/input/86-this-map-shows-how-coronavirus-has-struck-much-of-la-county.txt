Los Angeles County releases information daily about how many people in its cities and other communities have tested positive for coronavirus. Here is the latest as of Thursday, May 21. Health officials have been cautioning all along that the number of confirmed cases, and the list of locations, don’t show the disease’s true prevalence. Researchers working in L.A. County found in April that the number of people who have actually contracted COVID-19 was probably 28 to 55 times higher than the number of confirmed cases. As of Thursday, 42,138 people have tested positive for COVID-19 since the outbreak began, up 3% (1,163 cases) from the day before and 19% from a week before. That is based on the latest reports from L.A. County and the cities of Long Beach and Pasadena, which have their own health departments and are typically at least a day ahead of the county in reporting their cities’ numbers. Another 45 deaths were announced Thursday, bringing the county’s toll to 2,021. So far, about 403,000 people have been tested for the coronavirus in L.A. County. That represents 3.96% of the county’s population. County officials are releasing some additional information that does not include the cases in Long Beach and Pasadena. Hospitalizations: An average of 1,532 people per day have been hospitalized over the past three days. 6,026 patients have been hospitalized since the outbreak began. That’s 60 new hospitalizations reported since the day before.
